Muse Penguin Privacy Policy

Last updated November 26, 2026

Muse Penguin is made by Jason Muse. Your penguins live on your own devices. This page explains what the app keeps there, what it looks at, and the few things that ever leave your device.

The short version

  • Your colony, your settings and what you do in the game are kept on your device.
  • There are no ads, no tracking, and no analytics of our own in Muse Penguin.
  • A Muse account is optional. Without one, only a few things reach the internet: the jukebox's songs, feedback you choose to send, GIF searches if your version has GIFs, and on iPhone, update checks and Muse Pro.
  • With one, Muse gets what's needed to sign you in, check Muse Pro, keep your village so penguins can move between your devices, and run the Muse features you choose to use: friends, hops between your own computers, and your Muse Shelf.

On your computer (Windows and Mac)

What it keeps

Your colony, its story and your settings, in Muse Penguin's own folder (%APPDATA%\Muse Penguin on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Muse Penguin on Mac), which you can delete at any time.

What it looks at on your computer

  • Looking around (Settings → Let them look around my computer): now and then a penguin "digs up" a file on your desktop, in Downloads or in Music, or your computer's name. It sees the file's name (from which it can tell what kind of thing it is: a picture, a song, an FL Studio project) and, for a picture, song, video or document, your computer's own little thumbnail of it, squashed down to a tiny pixelated peek about a dozen pixels across. It never opens your files or reads what's in them beyond that. File names, those peeks and where things were found never leave your computer. Three small things can: your computer's name becomes this computer's label on your Muse account, if you're signed in; the name of a well-known game or app it recognized can be a search word for a GIF (see GIFs, below); and a penguin's memory of learning about that game or app can travel on a ticket you send a friend. Names that look private (banking, taxes, passwords and the like), emails, contact cards and calendars are left alone. You can switch it off.
  • Replays (Settings → Replays show what was behind them): the little replays in your notifications can show what was on your screen around the penguins at that moment, as a tiny, chunky pixelated snippet: shapes and colors, no writing you could read. It's kept with the replay on your computer and never leaves it. Switch it off and it's a plain sky.
  • Dancing to your music (Settings → Dance to my music): it listens to what your computer is playing to find the beat. The sound never leaves your computer, and you can switch it off.
  • Standing on your windows: it knows where your windows are, so the penguins can stand on them. Never their titles or what's in them.
  • Phone remote (optional, off until you switch it on): pairs a phone with your computer over your own network. Nothing goes through the internet.

On your iPhone

  • Kept on your phone: your colony and settings. The home screen and lock screen widgets and Live Activities read them on your phone.
  • Motion: the app uses your phone's motion sensor to know which way is down. That stays on your phone.
  • Notifications: they're scheduled on your phone itself. Nothing is sent anywhere to deliver them.
  • Updates: when it starts, the app checks for updates to the game with Expo, the service that delivers them. Like any website, they see your IP address and basic technical details such as the app version, plus a random ID for your install.
  • Muse Pro: purchases go through Apple and RevenueCat, the service that keeps track of who has Muse Pro. The app checks with RevenueCat when it starts and when you buy or restore. It uses a random ID from RevenueCat, or your Muse account ID once you're signed in, so one Muse Pro covers Muse Shelf and Muse Penguin. RevenueCat sees your Muse Pro purchase and whatever its own software sends, which its privacy policy covers. The app doesn't ask it for device identifiers or advertising data.
  • Crash reports: if you choose to share them with Apple, Apple's privacy policy covers them.

What reaches the internet without an account

  • The jukebox: once the radio's been found in the game, its songs are streamed from a Muse Labs site, muse-jukebox.pages.dev, while it plays. On your computer the app also fetches the list of songs about once a day. Nothing is sent but the request itself, so, like any website, it sees your IP address.
  • GIFs (later in the game, if your version has them; Settings → App → Privacy → Talk in GIFs): the penguins can talk in GIFs from GIPHY. To find one, the app sends one to three search words: words from a list that comes with the game ("happy dance"), and on your computer, the name of a well-known game or app they recognized (only while looking around is on) or the title of something on your Muse Shelf (only while they know your shelf). Never a file's name, and never anything you typed. GIPHY sees those words and your IP address. Switch it off and it stops.
  • Feedback: when you send a report from the app, Muse gets what you wrote, the kind of report, the app's version, which system it's on (such as Windows, Mac or iPhone), and a random ID kept on your device so you can see replies. If you're signed in, it's linked to your account. No logs, no screenshots.
  • On iPhone: update checks and Muse Pro, described above.

Your Muse account (optional)

It's the same account as Muse Shelf, run by Muse Labs on Supabase. You can play without one.

  • Signing in: with your email address and a one-time code, or with Google or Apple. Muse receives your email address and an account ID. Your sign-in is kept encrypted on your device.
  • Muse Pro: the app asks Muse whether your account has Pro. On iPhone it checks with RevenueCat too (see On your iPhone).
  • Your Muse Shelf (on your computer, with Pro, later in the game; Settings → They know what's on my shelf): the app reads the titles, types, status, ratings, genres and cover links of what's on your Shelf, read only, and keeps a copy on your device so a penguin can bring something up now and then. Covers are downloaded from where they're hosted.
  • Hops between your computers (with Pro, later in the game; not on iPhone yet): your devices running Muse Penguin (and Muse Shelf) meet on a private channel only your account can join. Each one shares a random device ID, which app it is, the kind of device and its label. A penguin that hops over travels whole, except its "Dug up" memories and anything it knows about your computer, and comes back after a few minutes. The app doesn't save anything from the hop itself.

Your village

While you're signed in, on a free account too, the app keeps your village on the Muse server, so your penguins can move between your devices.

  • Each device with the app open: a random device ID, the kind of device, when it was last seen, a label, and a short summary of up to 40 of its penguins (name, looks, personality, pronouns, birthday and parents). The label is "Muse Penguin on" your computer's name while looking around is on, otherwise just the kind of computer. An iPhone is always "Muse Penguin on iPhone".
  • A penguin between devices: the penguin itself (name, looks, personality, skills, bio and memories), without its "Dug up" memories or anything it knows about your computer, and which devices it's going to and coming from.
  • Asks between your devices ("send Milo here"): which devices, which penguin, and a short answer.
  • How long: a penguin's entry is deleted when it's back on a device. A device's entry is removed when the app closes normally, and cleared by itself if it hasn't been seen for 30 days. An ask goes when it's answered, or after an hour. Deleting your Muse account deletes all of it.

Muse friends (optional)

If you make a Muse profile, you can add friends, visit their colonies and show them yours. It follows the Muse community rules.

  • Your profile: your @handle, name, avatar, bio, and who can see it (just your friends, unless you choose everyone with Muse).
  • Friends: the friend requests you send and get, your friendships, the people you block, and any reports you make (with the details you add).
  • Your colony, for your friends: while "Friends can see my colony" is on, the app shares your colony's level, days and trophies, and each penguin's name, look, personality, pronouns, birthday and the little bio lines about them. Never your save. It shows to whoever can see your profile. Switch it off and it's taken down.
  • Keeping it friendly: names and bios pass a word filter, you can block or report anyone from their profile, and Muse reviews reports and may hide profiles that break the rules.

Tickets

Sending a penguin on a trip makes a ticket, a code or a file that you share yourself. No server is involved. It carries your colony's name and that penguin: their name, look, personality, a few of their stats and skills, and their latest memories, except the "Dug up" ones.

Children

Muse Penguin is not directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect personal information from them.

Your choices

You can play without a Muse account, switch off looking around, listening and GIFs in Settings, take your colony down or block and report anyone in Friends, and sign out at any time. To delete your Muse account and everything Muse keeps about it, delete it right in Muse Penguin (Settings → App → Muse account → Delete my Muse account) or in Muse Shelf, or email us. Deleting the app deletes your colony on that device.
